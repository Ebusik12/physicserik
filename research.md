My current research is in levitational optomechanics and precision measurement of trapped micromechanical systems. On this page you can find a short overview of my work on [optical levitation](#optical-levitation) and [feedback control & measurement](#control-and-measurement).

## Optical levitation and trapping {#optical-levitation}
#### Experiment design and control

I build and characterize laboratory systems that trap small particles using light and electromagnetic feedback. The work focuses on stabilizing levitated objects in vacuum, reducing environmental noise, and translating optical forces into reliable experimental control.

#### Precision measurement

Precise readout of position and motion is critical for levitational optomechanics. I use interferometric sensing, balanced detection, and custom data acquisition pipelines to measure displacements at the nanometer scale and to quantify the stability of suspended systems.

#### Data and analysis

Experimental data are processed with Python and custom measurement software to extract mechanical spectra, noise limits, and control loop performance. This includes filtering, spectral analysis, and modeling of trap dynamics, as well as the automated extraction of key parameters from time-series recordings.

## Feedback control and experimental systems {#control-and-measurement}

#### Active stabilization

Feedback loops are implemented to keep levitated particles centered and to suppress low-frequency drift. The systems combine optical trapping with real-time control signals, digital actuation, and precise calibration of force response.

#### Instrumentation

The setup uses vacuum-compatible optics, high-speed photodetectors, programmable signal generators, and data acquisition hardware. Careful alignment, thermal control, and acoustic isolation help ensure experiments are reproducible and robust.

### Key takeaways

- Levitation enables contact-free mechanical testing and access to low-dissipation dynamics.  
- Precision readout is essential for characterizing optomechanical coupling and feedback performance.  
- Active control improves stability and opens the door to new experiments in levitational optomechanics.
